1 · Investigate Critical Epitopes

Single antibodies can cause disease. Using a HuProt protein microarray, Lanz et al. found that a monoclonal antibody isolated from a multiple sclerosis patient binds the human protein HEPACAM (GlialCAM). Molecular mimicry with Epstein-Barr virus is thought to underlie this cross-reactivity. The calculation walkthrough explains how this match is mapped.

Here, we compare the HEPACAM sequence from a HuProt protein microarray experiment (JHU19101; Q14CZ8) with viral proteins and polyproteins from Curated Viral Proteomes (120). The known 5/9 match is present but does not stand out by sequence similarity alone. The authors knew to focus on EBV due to recent work connecting it with MS, and had separate PhIP-Seq experiments with this same antibody demonstrating binding to proline-rich peptides similar to this match site within HEPACAM. They distinguished this site further by showing phosphorylation of GlialCAM serine 376 (pSer376) increased binding affinity of the tested antibody about 50-fold.

2 · Calculate Reactivity Enrichment

Multiplex serology platforms such as HuProt and VirScan PhIP-Seq can generate thousands of antibody reactivity calls per sample. Each antigen has a source and metadata label, but cross-reactivity to similar sequences within and across species complicates interpretation.

MimicryMap applies sequence-identity and coverage thresholds to compare assay antigens and proteomes. Here it maps HuProt and VirScan assay antigens to 120 canonical viral proteomes, defining antigen sets independently of the sample’s reactions. A downstream Reactivity Enrichment analysis tests whether recorded antibody hits concentrate in each set relative to the sample’s overall assay hit rate.

Worked Example: Calls Within a Single Sample

This sample was measured with VirScan. Its assay peptides represent proteins from human and non-human viruses. The assay processing recorded 954 above-background reactions across 481,962 measured peptides.

To summarize these reactions against canonical virus strains, the measured peptides are linked to the MimicryMap summaries for Curated Viral Proteomes (120). Here, assignment requires at least 7 of 9 amino acids to match within short sequence cores, with qualifying cores covering at least 60% of the full assay peptide. This assigns 1,798 measured peptides to respiratory syncytial virus A (strain A2), including homologous assay peptides. The sample reacted to 156 of those 1,798 peptides. We compare this set’s hit rate with the sample’s overall assay hit rate, which supplies the expected-by-chance baseline for Reactivity Enrichment. A one-sided Fisher test assesses excess hits relative to the sample’s overall assay hit rate; the same per-sample enrichment values feed cohort summaries.

Calls Across a Cohort of Samples: VirScan

The calculation is repeated separately for each sample in a study. The figure summarizes VirScan enrichment across 35 human samples, showing which viral sets consistently have more hits than expected by chance. Sample BH identifies reactive sets within each sample; a one-sided sign test and Group BH assess consistency above 1× across the cohort.

Calls Across a Cohort of Samples: HuProt

HuProt applies this analysis to autoantibodies against human proteins. Matching these antigens to the same 120 viral proteomes asks whether reactivity concentrates among human proteins resembling particular viruses. Across this cohort, recorded autoantibody hits are enriched among human HuProt proteins containing EBV-matching sequence segments above the selected identity and coverage thresholds.

3 · Discover Mimicry Across Proteomes

Viruses can evolve molecular mimics of host proteins. Comparing coverage in both directions helps distinguish similarity spanning much of a protein from smaller, localized matches.

Here, the target is human IL10, an immunoregulatory cytokine. Epstein-Barr virus encodes an evolved homolog, BCRF1, that mimics human IL10 activity. Other viruses, including human cytomegalovirus, also encode functional IL10 mimics. The full extent and mechanisms of viral mimicry remain incompletely understood; these comparisons highlight candidates for investigation, including less extensive similarities whose biological significance is unknown.

4 · Find Uniqueness Within Proteomes

Avoiding unwanted cross-reactivity is important for drug design and the selection of monoclonal antibody reagents. However, distinctive regions can be difficult to find. The mapping system described here identifies regions unmatched within a chosen comparison population, such as across the entire human proteome.

In this example, IGHG2, the constant region of the IgG2 heavy chain, is compared with the human proteome at ≥8/9 core identity. Only one short stretch is unmatched at this threshold; the remaining regions closely resemble other IgG subclasses such as IgG1, IgG3, and IgG4.
